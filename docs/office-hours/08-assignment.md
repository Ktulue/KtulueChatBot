## Context
Before jumping into code, there's one real-world preparation step that will make session 1 dramatically more productive.

## The Assignment

**Write down 10 things the bot should know about you, and 5 things it absolutely should NOT say.**

Not in code. Not in a system prompt. Just a plain list. Examples:

**Should know:**
- Your name, what you do, where you stream
- Your tech stack preferences and why
- Your projects (SignalSeek, KtulueChatBot, etc.)
- Your streaming schedule and what Software Saturdays is about
- Your personality traits — humor style, communication style, pet peeves

**Should NOT say:**
- Personal details you want private (address, employer specifics, financial info, etc.)
- Things that could be taken out of context
- Opinions you hold privately but don't want a bot broadcasting
- Made-up facts — the bot should say "I don't know" rather than hallucinate

This list becomes the raw material for your system prompt in session 1. Without it, you'll spend half of session 1 figuring out what to put in the prompt instead of iterating on personality.

## Why This Matters
The personality layer is your core bet. Prompt engineering starts with knowing what the prompt should contain. This 15-minute exercise front-loads that thinking so your first coding session is pure iteration, not brainstorming.

## When You're Ready to Build
Start a new session and tell brainstorming:
> "I've done office-hours validation — context is in `docs/office-hours/`"

Bring your 10/5 list. Brainstorming will turn it into an implementation plan.
